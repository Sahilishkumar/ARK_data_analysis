ARK Donation Analytics: Key Findings

1. Education leads funding allocation
Education received the highest share of donations by cause, followed by child care and animal care. This suggests strong donor interest in education focused initiatives, making it a promising area for future campaigns.


2. Male donors dominate contributions
Male donors account for a substantially larger share of donation records and total contributions than female donors. The college's gender ratio may be one possible explanation, but this needs validation against the actual donor base before drawing conclusions.


3. December 2025 saw the highest fundraising activity
December recorded the highest donation amount, potentially driven by the Celebrate with a Cause birthday initiative. This suggests that occasion based campaigns can be an effective fundraising strategy.


4. Payment rejection rate is approximately 1%
Around 99% of the donation records were approved, indicating a high payment approval rate. The low rejection rate is encouraging, although rejected payments alone cannot establish the level of fraud or attempted fraud.


5. Fundraising recovered after an initial decline
Donation amounts declined after December 2025, reached a low in May 2026, and recovered in July and August. This suggests renewed fundraising momentum, although the reasons for the recovery require further investigation.


6. Campaign performance is concentrated
Gift a School Kit generated 17 donation records in July and 14 in August, while Orphanage Visit Drive recorded 21 in March. Replicating successful campaign formats and studying their outreach channels could help improve future results.


7. Small donations can drive meaningful participation
The prevalence of smaller contributions is consistent with ARK's microdonation model. Tracking repeat donors, average donation size, and conversion from campaign views to donations could help identify ways to grow fundraising without relying only on large contributions.
